Stall types

The basics

What Is an Exhibition Stall?

An exhibition stall, also called a stand or booth, is the space an exhibitor builds or rents inside a show hall to present products and meet visitors. At most shows you pay the organizer for the floor space and then decide how that space will be built.

There are two separate choices to make: how the stall is built (shell scheme, custom or modular) and how the space sits on the floor (inline, corner, peninsula or island).

Build types

Four Ways to Build a Stall

Build typeWhat it isBest for
Shell schemeStandard walls, fascia and basic fittings supplied by the organizer. You add graphics and furniture.First-time or low-budget exhibitors at shows that offer it
Custom stallDesigned and fabricated for your brand, size and spaceExhibitors who want a distinctive look and a stand built around their goals
Modular stallReusable panel and frame system that can be reconfigured between showsExhibitors who attend several events a year
Two-storey or large custom buildA multi-level custom structure, often with a private meeting area upstairsFlagship activations at larger shows

If shell scheme is on offer, ask the organizer whether the space is bare or shell, because that changes how much you need to build. Our custom vs modular guide covers the main decision in detail.

Floor layouts

Stall Layouts by Number of Open Sides

01

Inline

One open side facing the aisle, with neighbours on both sides. The most economical position, so the design has to work hard on a narrow frontage.

02

Corner

Two open sides, at the end of a row. Visitors see the stand from two directions, which helps with visibility.

03

Peninsula

Three open sides, with one side against a neighbour or wall. Gives strong visibility and room for a counter on the open frontage.

04

Island

Open on all four sides. Maximum visibility and flexibility, with the highest space cost and more branding surfaces to design.

Choosing

How to Choose the Right Type

  1. Start with your goal: lead capture, product demos, buyer meetings or brand visibility each need different layouts.
  2. Count your shows: if you exhibit often, a reusable modular booth can lower the cost per show.
  3. Check your position: your layout is set by where the organizer places you. Ask which sides are open before you design.
  4. Set a budget range: use the Pakistan cost guide or the Karachi cost guide to see what each size and build type typically costs.
  5. Confirm the size: see our stall size guide for what fits in 9, 18 and 36 sqm.

Questions

Types of Exhibition Stalls FAQ

In practice the terms mean the same thing. “Stall” and “stand” are common in Pakistan and the UK, while “booth” is more common in North America.

A standard package from the organizer, usually basic walls, a fascia name board and simple fittings. Exhibitors add graphics, furniture and products.

Shell scheme is normally the lowest upfront cost. A modular stall costs more upfront but can work out cheaper if you reuse it across several shows.

An island stall is open on all four sides, so visitors can approach from every aisle that surrounds it.
