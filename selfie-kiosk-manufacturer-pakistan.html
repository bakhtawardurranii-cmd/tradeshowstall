The maths nobody shows you

A Selfie Kiosk Is a Queue Machine With a Camera

Most buyers choose a selfie kiosk by screen size and frame style. That is the wrong starting point. A selfie kiosk earns or wastes your money in seconds per guest. If one session takes 55 seconds, one unit can serve at best about 65 people an hour, and in real life closer to 40–50 because people hesitate, retake and chat. If 120 people an hour want a photo, half of them will watch a queue and leave.

The cheapest way to add capacity is rarely a second kiosk. It is shaving 15 seconds off the session: a three-second countdown instead of five, one retake instead of unlimited, and a share step that is a QR scan rather than typing a phone number on a glass screen.

Light is the product

Why Most Selfie Kiosk Photos Look Bad (and the Fix)

Guests judge the kiosk by how they look in the first photo. Three lighting failures cause nearly every bad result:

  • Overhead venue spotlights. They throw shadows under the eyes and nose. The fix is a soft, frontal light at roughly face height that is stronger than the light above.
  • Window or glass behind the guest. The camera exposes for the bright background and the face turns dark. Place the kiosk so the brightest thing in view is the branded backdrop, not a window.
  • Mixed colour temperatures. Warm shop lights plus cool LEDs make skin look patchy. Many designers choose a neutral-white light source and shield the guest from other lights with a hood or side panels.

A hood is not decoration. It controls what light reaches the face. That is why we design the light housing as part of the structure rather than clipping on a ring light afterwards.

The pain points

Seven Things That Go Wrong After the Kiosk Is Delivered

01

Venue wi-fi fails

Photos that must upload before sharing stop working in crowded halls. Choose software that works offline and syncs later.

02

Shared screens feel unhygienic

Offer touch-free options such as a foot pedal, a countdown that starts on motion or a QR code the guest scans on their own phone.

03

Nobody knows where to stand

A floor mark and an on-screen outline fix framing mistakes and cut retakes.

04

Branding is too small

Your logo should be in every photo without blocking faces. A framed backdrop does this better than a watermark.

05

Guests cannot reach it

Check height for children and wheelchair users, and let the camera angle adjust.

06

Data without consent

If you collect numbers or emails, show a short consent line. It builds trust and protects you.

07

No one owns it

Name one person per shift who restarts, refills paper and clears queues. Unattended kiosks fail quietly.

Before you buy

Ten Questions to Ask Any Selfie Kiosk Supplier, Including Us

  1. How many seconds does one full session take, measured, not estimated?
  2. What happens if the internet drops?
  3. How is the guest lit, and how is venue light blocked?
  4. How does a child or a wheelchair user take a photo?
  5. How does sharing work, and how many taps does it take?
  6. Where does the data go, and who can see it?
  7. What does the kiosk need: power points, floor space, ventilation?
  8. How is it transported and how long does assembly take?
  9. Who fixes it during the event?
  10. What can be reused for the next campaign?

These questions also tell you whether you need a selfie kiosk at all. If the answer to the first one is longer than a minute, consider a staffed photo wall instead.
